## The commercial opportunity: services attached to a verifiable pain, not AI enthusiasm

Rankings below are **analyst prioritization** for a low-capital solo web developer or small professional-services team, not calculated market-size projections or promised income.

**Do not equate prize winnings with sustainable recurring revenue.** Polyfork's $4,428 first-month gross receipts are a useful signal of market contact, but not sufficient to fund a full-time business without margin, retention and repeat purchase data. The winner's $500k competition prize is not operating income from customers.

### Two cross-cutting forecasting lessons

1. **Projected performance is not measured deployment.** In #285, “3.4×” is modeled future ASIC behavior while inference on an FPGA is measured. An 80,000-year trip is a planned mission, not launched travel. A breakthrough mathematical announcement may be apparently successful but still require community validation. All three should be tracked as a distinct `claim_stage`: `proposal`, `simulation`, `physical_demo`, `third_party_validation`, `scaled_deployment`, or `regulatory_approval` where applicable.
2. **Audience selection is not representative sampling.** Moonshots tends to select optimistic, newsworthy cases. Prize finalists are selected from tens of thousands of participants. A security executive's anecdote has no control group. The right research procedure records denominator, counterfactual and negative evidence before deriving a forecast.

## Concrete two-week experimental plan (not automatic)

**Days 1–2:** Choose ONE existing agent workflow with repeatable runs. Freeze version, select 10–20 representative non-sensitive tasks, create baseline outcomes and count manual interventions.  
**Days 3–5:** Instrument action log, per-task max spend/time, retries, explicit approval for irreversible actions, versioned prompts, regression test and rollback. No extra multi-agent architecture.  
**Days 6–8:** Replay comparable inputs with validation by a second reviewer; publish elapsed time, correctness rate and failure-recovery time, including negative results.  
**Days 9–10:** Write a short screenshot-supported case study and a fixed-scope implementation offer (do not expose client secrets).  
**Days 11–14:** Seek permissioned feedback from three relevant operators, preferably asynchronously; if no one recognizes a paid problem, revise offer instead of building more features.  
**Decision rule:** Proceed only if measured internal value **and** buyer interest. No guarantee of conversion or income.
