## Pilot: episode #300, Astro Teller at Moonshots Live

The publicly visible excerpt contains three connected ideas:
- **Define testable moonshots:** name a significant problem, a radical solution, and a technology breakthrough that could make the solution feasible. [Episode #300 excerpt](https://spoken.md/episode/google-xs-astro-teller-the-$1b-bet-no-ceo-will-back-1000793315034), starting ~01:18. The same framework appears in [X's own blueprint](https://x.company/blog/posts/moonshot-blueprint/). This is **primary-source corroboration**, not independent external verification.
- **Use audacity together with humility:** test risky assumptions early, cheaply and dispassionately; abandon losers rather than over-investing. Episode ~02:00, consistent with [X's operating manual](https://x.company/blog/posts/a-peek-inside-the-moonshot-factory-operating-manual/).
- **Kill projects for economic reasons too:** even promising technology may lack workable unit economics. Episode ~03:36–04:31; X's operating manual details the Foghorn project's techno-economic failure.

### First practical hypothesis (not a podcast prediction)

For a small software/services business, require a **one-page kill-test before any multi-week build**: defined problem and buyer, evidence that the buyer will pay, cheapest meaningful experiment, target success metric, and stop criterion. This is a low-cost, reversible recommendation inferred from methodology, **not proof that it increases revenue**. Test on one prospective offer and record outcome before scaling.

### Still unverified / not yet forecasted

The episode titles mention recursive self-improving AI, safety policies, robotics, datacenter energy, and investor forecasts. **Titles alone cannot establish these claims.** The research queue prioritizes source transcript acquisition, independent documents, opposing evidence and date-specific resolution criteria before publishing a forecast or numeric probability.
